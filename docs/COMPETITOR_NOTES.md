# Competitor Notes: NeatEat

*Reviewed 22 Nov 2026. Source: the public pages at neateat.app (homepage including "How it works" and "Features", Recipes, Guides index and one sample article). We did not use the app itself, so everything below is about its marketing and content, not how it actually behaves.*

> These notes are for feature and layout inspiration only. We do not copy NeatEat's branding, wording, recipes or article content.

## What NeatEat is

A US budgeting meal planner with iPhone and Android apps and a marketing site. The pitch is "plan a week of meals that fits your budget."

## How the product is presented

**Three-step flow on the homepage:** pick your store, set your weekly budget, get a 7-day plan.
- Stores shown: Walmart, Kroger, Aldi, Target, Trader Joe's, Costco. Prices are in US dollars.
- The example shows a weekly budget slider and a plan that comes in under budget.

**Six feature cards:** budget meal plans, a recipe library, a recipe organiser (import from anywhere, sort into cookbooks), a grocery list built from the plan, diets and allergies, and calorie and macro targets.

**Grocery list:** grouped by aisle (produce, meat, dairy and eggs, pantry), shows the total against the budget, and has a "compare" link for stores.

**FAQ:** free to start; you can accept suggested meals, add your own, or mix both; it says it is not a nutrition or health tracker.

## Recipes page
- A searchable library with filters for meal type (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack) and diet (everyday, vegetarian, vegan, pescatarian, low carb, high protein, Mediterranean).
- Each card shows time, calories and servings. Most recipes serve 4.
- The page lists about 2,400 recipes, compared with "1,000+" in the homepage marketing.

## Guides
- About 41 articles, all published within two days under one author byline.
- Topics: budget meal plans for different households, grocery budgeting, meal prep, food waste, general budgeting, and diet-specific budget plans.
- The sample article is long (10 minutes) and structured: a short summary, a numbered method, a full costed 7-day plan, a priced shopping list, mistakes to avoid, an FAQ, and repeated prompts to try the app.
- Titles are search-style questions, such as "how to meal plan on a budget" and "how to save money on groceries". This is how the site attracts search traffic.

## Observations
- **Mixed messages:** the homepage lists calorie and macro targets as a feature, while the FAQ says the app doesn't track nutrition.
- **Library, not generation:** plans are "picked from" a fixed library. There's no sign of ingredient-based generation, partial ingredients or smart substitutions.
- **Price source unclear:** the site doesn't say where its store prices come from. Guide prices are in dollars, and the content never explains where its prices come from.
- **US-only:** US stores and currency.
- **Their own article admits a limit:** it says published "cheapest store" lists go out of date and suggests readers build a short personal price list. That supports our fallback of letting users enter prices.

## What this suggests for our product

| Idea | Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Three-step "store, budget, plan" flow | Worth copying as a pattern. It's clear and easy to explain. Do it with UK stores and pounds. |
| Grocery list grouped by aisle, with total vs budget | Include it in Phase 3. |
| Recipe organiser and import | Start with "paste a link". Share-sheet import needs a native app. |
| Guides as a search-traffic engine | Good fit for Phase 4, but each article needs checked UK prices. Don't publish unchecked AI-written money advice. |
| Our difference: generate recipes from what you have | Their library approach can't do this. Lead with "use up what's in your kitchen" in our marketing. |
| Our difference: hard allergy rules | They say meals "respect what you avoid" but explain nothing. We can show our approach clearly. |
| Our difference: UK stores and prices | The main gap versus NeatEat, but we still need real UK price data. |

## What this doesn't tell us
- Whether NeatEat's plans really come in under budget, and where its prices come from.
- Pricing after the free start, or how its app feels to use.
- Whether any UK competitor already exists. The "no UK competitor" claim is still unverified, and we need a separate UK search.
